Why Convolutions?

Replacing brute-force dense connectivity with locality, weight sharing, and shift equivariance.

Why Dense Layers Fail on Visual Signals

Suppose we feed a $256 \times 256$ RGB image ($\vec{x} \in \RR^{196{,}608}$) into a single dense hidden layer of $1{,}000$ units:
  • Parameter Explosion: $196{,}608 \times 1{,}000 \approx \mathbf{1.97 \times 10^8}$ weights in layer 1 alone!
  • Zero Shift Generalization: Moving a cat 3 pixels right routes through completely different weights.
  • Spatial Locality Ignored: Neighboring pixels and opposite corners are treated identically.
Convolutions encode two structural priors: spatial locality and stationarity (shared detectors).

From Dense Matrix to Toeplitz Weight Sharing

A 1D convolution $\vec{y} = \bm{K}\vec{x}$ is simply a linear map $\vec{y} = \bm{W}\vec{x}$ with two constraints on $\bm{W}$:
Reverse-Mode AD Link (Lecture 18): Because $(a,b,c)$ fan out across rows in $\bm{W}\vec{x}$, backprop sums gradients over space: $\displaystyle \frac{\partial {\cal L}}{\partial w_u} = \sum_i \frac{\partial {\cal L}}{\partial y_i} x_{i+u}$ and $\displaystyle \frac{\partial {\cal L}}{\partial \vec{x}} = \bm{W}^\top \frac{\partial {\cal L}}{\partial \vec{y}}$!

Discrete Convolution vs. Cross-Correlation

1. Convolution ($*$): flips kernel $180^\circ$
$(I * K)[i,j] = \sum_{u,v} I[i - u,\, j - v]\, K[u, v]$
2. Cross-Correlation ($\star$): DL Conv default
$(I \star K)[i,j] = \sum_{u,v} I[i + u,\, j + v]\, K[u, v]$
Why DL skips the flip: Since $\bm{K}$ is learned, $\bm{K}$ vs. $\bm{K}_{\text{flip}}$ span the exact same hypothesis space — and the flipped kernel $*$ reappears automatically in $\bm{W}^\top$ during backprop!
Watch both landmarks sweep across n=0..14: at the unit impulse (n=3), Convolution (*) outputs the upright kernel K while Cross-Correlation (⋆) flips it; over the asymmetric ramp (n=9..11), Cross-Correlation aligns template to signal and produces the matched-filter peak! Drag slider n or click anywhere on the signal to inspect a single step.

Translation Equivariance vs. Invariance

Let $T_{\Delta}$ shift an image by $\Delta = (\Delta_x, \Delta_y)$: $[T_{\Delta}\bm{X}]_{i,j} = X_{i-\Delta_x,\, j-\Delta_y}$.
$\displaystyle \big((T_{\Delta}\bm{X}) \star \bm{K}\big)[i,j] = \sum_{u,v} X_{i - \Delta_x + u,\, j - \Delta_y + v}\, K_{u,v} = \big[T_{\Delta}(\bm{X} \star \bm{K})\big]_{i,j}$

History of CNNs

From cat visual cortex (1962) to Neocognitron (1980), LeNet (1998), and ImageNet GPUs (2012).

Cat Visual Cortex: Hubel & Wiesel (1962)

Hubel & Wiesel (1962): Receptive fields, binocular interaction and functional architecture in the cat's visual cortex
Simple cells act as localized oriented edge detectors; complex cells aggregate simple-cell responses across small spatial neighborhoods for local shift tolerance.

Fukushima's Neocognitron (1979 / 1980)

Fukushima (1980): Neocognitron — A Self-organizing Neural Network Model for Pattern Recognition Unaffected by Shift in Position
Introduced alternating S-cells (simple feature extractors) and C-cells (complex spatial pooling). Trained via self-organization rather than end-to-end backpropagation.

Time Delay Neural Network: Waibel et al. (1989)

Waibel et al. (1989): Phoneme Recognition Using Time-Delay Neural Networks
Note 1D convolutions across time with frequency channels — shared weight matrices trained with backpropagation!

Backprop-Trained CNN: LeCun et al. (1989)

LeCun et al. (1989): Backpropagation Applied to Handwritten Zip Code Recognition
First end-to-end gradient-trained 2D convolutional network! Note strided subsampling (step=2) to halve spatial resolution across layers.

LeNet-5 for Document Recognition (1998)

Alternating Conv $\to$ Subsample hierarchy, deployed to read millions of handwritten checks in production.
LeCun et al. (1998): Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition
Note average subsampling with learned scale+bias (no MaxPooling yet) and 5x5 convolutions.

GPU CNNs + MaxPooling: Cireşan et al. (2011)

Cireşan et al. (2011): High-Performance Neural Networks for Visual Object Classification
Everything clicks here: Max Pooling, fast CUDA GPU kernels (60x speedup vs CPU), and large datasets — winning Chinese handwriting, Traffic Sign recognition (2011), and Neuronal Membrane segmentation (2012).

AlexNet & the ImageNet Breakthrough (2012)

Ingredients: ReLU activations, dual GPUs, overlapping MaxPool, data augmentation & Dropout — slashing ImageNet Top-5 error from $26.2\%$ to $15.3\%$.
Krizhevsky, Sutskever & Hinton (NeurIPS 2012): ImageNet Classification with Deep Convolutional Neural Networks

Zero Padding, Stride & Output Geometry

Multi-Channel Tensors & Volumetric Convolution

Mapping $\bm{X} \in \RR^{C_{\text{in}} \times H \times W} \to \bm{Y} \in \RR^{C_{\text{out}} \times H' \times W'}$ uses a 4D filter tensor $\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}} \in \RR^{C_{\text{out}} \times C_{\text{in}} \times k \times k}$:
$\displaystyle Y_{m, i, j} = b_m + \sum_{c=1}^{C_{\text{in}}} \sum_{u,v} X_{c,\, i+u,\, j+v}\, \mathcal{W}_{m, c, u, v} \qquad \text{Parameters} = C_{\text{out}}\,(C_{\text{in}}\, k^2 + 1)$
Each filter spans all $C_{\text{in}}$ input channels (or 3D depth)
$C_{\text{out}}$ distinct filters produce $C_{\text{out}}$ feature maps

Spatial Pooling: Max vs. Average

MaxPool: keeps strongest feature activation in window
AvgPool: smooth spatial summary (or Global Avg Pool at top)
Downsampling shrinks memory and grows receptive fields — at the cost of fine spatial localization!

From Feature Maps to Class Predictions

1. Flatten + Dense MLP Head (AlexNet / VGG)
$7 \times 7 \times 512 \to 4096 \to 1000$ classes.
Uses 102.7M parameters ($74\%$ of VGG-16!) and locks network to a fixed input resolution.
2. Global Average Pooling + Linear Head (ResNet)
$\displaystyle \bar{z}_c = \frac{1}{HW}\sum_{i=1}^H \sum_{j=1}^W F_{c, i, j}$, then $\text{softmax}(\bm{W}\bar{\vec{z}} + \vec{b})$.
0 pooling parameters, natural regularizer, works on arbitrary $H \times W$!
Lin, Chen & Yan (2013): Network In Network (Global Average Pooling)

1×1 Convolution: Per-Pixel Channel MLP

At each pixel $(i,j)$, apply a shared linear map across channels:
$\vec{y}_{i,j} = \bm{W}\vec{x}_{i,j} + \vec{b}, \qquad \bm{W} \in \RR^{C_{\text{out}} \times C_{\text{in}}}$
Channel Mixing & Bottlenecks:
Shrink $256 \to 64$ before $3\times 3$ convs ($\sim 9\times$ fewer FLOPs) and combine cross-channel features cheaply.
Lin, Chen & Yan (2013): Network In Network

Transposed Convolution (Upconvolution)

Forward strided conv multiplies by sparse Toeplitz $\bm{W}$:
Upconv multiplies by $\bm{W}^\top$ (exact backward operator!)
$H_{\text{out}} = (H_{\text{in}} - 1)s - 2p + k$
  • Not mathematical deconvolution ($\bm{W}^{-1}$), just learnable spatial upsampling!
  • Checkerboard trap: if $k$ is not divisible by $s$, uneven overlaps create artifacts (fix: resize + conv).
Odena, Dumoulin & Olah (Distill 2016): Deconvolution and Checkerboard Artifacts

Dilated Convolution: Exponential Context

Insert $d-1$ zeros between kernel taps — keeping only $k^2$ weights!
$R_{\ell} = R_{\ell-1} + (k_{\ell} - 1)\, d_{\ell} \prod_{m < \ell} s_m$
  • $d = 1, 2, 4, 8 \implies$ RF grows exponentially ($3 \to 7 \to 15 \to 31$).
  • Preserves full $100\%$ spatial resolution without pooling or skip-buffer RAM!
Yu & Koltun (ICLR 2016): Multi-Scale Context Aggregation by Dilated Convolutions

Summary: Eight Core CNN Primitives

1. Shared Kernel Conv: Toeplitz weight sharing ($\sim k^2$ params) + translation equivariance.
2. Padding ($p$) & Stride ($s$): Valid/Same/Full border control & downsampling geometry.
3. Multi-Channel 4D Tensors: Every output filter mixes all $C_{\text{in}}$ input channels.
4. Pooling & GAP: Spatial downsampling & parameter-free class-head aggregation.
5. $1 \times 1$ Convolution: Per-pixel channel MLP for bottleneck compute savings.
6. Transposed Conv ($\bm{W}^\top$): Learnable spatial upsampling (watch checkerboard artifacts).
7. Dilated Conv ($d$): Exponential receptive field at full spatial resolution.
8. End-to-End Composition: Hierarchies from Gabor edges $\to$ parts $\to$ semantics.

Skip Connections

How identity shortcuts unlock 100+ layer networks via Reverse-Mode AD.
Thanks to Speelpenning's reverse-mode accumulation (Lecture 18), backpropagation walks backward along every path in the computation graph. Adding an un-attenuated identity edge turns out to be transformative for training very deep models.

The Depth Paradox & Dark Knowledge

Why couldn't we train deep plain nets before 2015?
  • Degradation Paradox: Deeper plain nets had higher training error — an optimization barrier, not overfitting!
  • Mimic Nets & Dark Knowledge (2014–15): Train a thin student on a shallow teacher's softened probabilities (same Temperature $T$ from Lecture 20!): $$q_i = \frac{\exp(z_i / T)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j / T)}$$
  • FitNets (2015): Intermediate feature hints let deep thin nets train — but still needed two-stage teacher distillation!
Ba & Caruana (2014): Do Deep Nets Really Need to be Deep? · Hinton et al. (2015): Distilling the Knowledge · Romero et al. (2015): FitNets
Rich Caruana (2013/14) noticed shallow mimic nets could match deep ensembles; Geoff Hinton named the rich inter-class probability ratios in soft logits "dark knowledge". FitNets used intermediate hints, yet multi-stage distillation remained cumbersome.

Highway Networks: Learned Carry Gates (May 2015)

  • $$\vec{y} = H(\vec{x}, \bm{W}_H) \odot T(\vec{x}, \bm{W}_T) + \vec{x} \odot C(\vec{x}, \bm{W}_C)$$
  • Tie carry gate $C = 1 - T$ with sigmoid transform gate $T(\vec{x}, \bm{W}_T) = \sigma(\bm{W}_T \vec{x} + \vec{b}_T)$: $$\vec{y} = H(\vec{x}, \bm{W}_H) \odot T(\vec{x}, \bm{W}_T) + \vec{x} \odot \big(1 - T(\vec{x}, \bm{W}_T)\big)$$
  • $$\vec{y} = \begin{cases} \vec{x} & \text{if } T(\vec{x}, \bm{W}_T) = 0 \quad (\text{pure identity skip!}) \\ H(\vec{x}, \bm{W}_H) & \text{if } T(\vec{x}, \bm{W}_T) = 1 \quad (\text{nonlinear transform}) \end{cases}$$
  • Key Initialization Trick: set gate bias $\vec{b}_T \ll 0$ so $T \approx 0$ at step 0 and gradients flow freely!
Srivastava, Greff & Schmidhuber (May 2015): Highway Networks · Training Very Deep Networks (NeurIPS 2015)

Training 100 Layers with Highway Gates

Train models with 100+ layers directly end-to-end instead of collapsing past 10–20!
Srivastava, Greff & Schmidhuber (2015): Training Very Deep Networks

Residual Networks: The Identity Highway (Dec 2015)

Drop the learned gate completely ($C \equiv 1, T \equiv 1$): learn residual $\mathcal{F}(\vec{x}) = \mathcal{H}(\vec{x}) - \vec{x}$!
$\vec{x}_{\ell+1} = \vec{x}_\ell + \mathcal{F}(\vec{x}_\ell, \bm{W}_\ell) \implies \vec{x}_L = \vec{x}_\ell + \sum_{k=\ell}^{L-1} \mathcal{F}(\vec{x}_k, \bm{W}_k)$
$\displaystyle \frac{\partial {\cal L}}{\partial \vec{x}_\ell} = \frac{\partial {\cal L}}{\partial \vec{x}_L}\!\left(\underbrace{\bm{I}}_{\text{Direct AD Highway!}} + \;\frac{\partial}{\partial \vec{x}_\ell}\sum_{k=\ell}^{L-1}\mathcal{F}(\vec{x}_k, \bm{W}_k)\right)$
Zero extra gate parameters; paired with BatchNorm for stable activations across 152+ layers.
He et al. (Dec 2015 / CVPR 2016): Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition

DenseNet: How Channel Concatenation Stays Small

ResNet adds: $\vec{x}_\ell = \vec{x}_{\ell-1} + \mathcal{F}_\ell(\vec{x}_{\ell-1})$
DenseNet concatenates: $\vec{x}_\ell = \mathcal{H}_\ell\!\big([\vec{x}_0, \vec{x}_1, \dots, \vec{x}_{\ell-1}]\big)$
Channel count grows linearly ($C_\ell = C_0 + \ell g$), so raw weights would grow as $O(L^2 g^2)$ — 3 tricks tame it:
1. Tiny Growth Rate ($g = 12\text{–}32$): Each layer only emits $g$ new channels instead of wide $256\text{–}512$ maps!
2. $1\times 1$ Bottleneck (DenseNet-B): Compresses all $C_\ell$ concatenated channels down to $4g$ before each $3\times 3$ conv ($4g \to g$).
3. Transition Layers (DenseNet-C): Between blocks, $1\times 1$ conv ($\theta = 0.5$) + AvgPool halves channels ($C \to 0.5C$).
Huang et al. (CVPR 2017): Densely Connected Convolutional Networks

Causal autoregressive masks, deformable sampling, depthwise separability & modern frontier roles.

Autoregressive Factoring & Masked Convolutions

Turn image generation into exact chain-rule density estimation in raster-scan order: $\quad \displaystyle p(\bm{x}) = \prod_{i=1}^{H \cdot W} p(x_i \mid x_1, \dots, x_{i-1})$
  • Multiply kernel weights by binary mask $\bm{M}$: $\bm{W}_{\text{causal}} = \bm{W} \odot \bm{M}$ strictly zeroes connections from future pixels.
  • Why Mask A vs. Mask B? Layer 1 must block center pixel $x_i$ itself so the model cannot predict $x_i = x_i$ trivially; deeper layers can read center features via Mask B!
  • RGB channels: Mask B also masks $R \to G \to B$ sequentially at the same pixel.
van den Oord et al. (ICML 2016): Pixel Recurrent Neural Networks

PixelCNN: Parallel Training & Blind-Spot Fix

Training ($O(1)$ Passes) vs. Sampling ($O(HW)$ Passes)
Training: Because ground truth $\bm{x}$ is known, one masked conv pass evaluates all $H \cdot W$ conditionals in parallel!
Sampling: Must generate one pixel at a time left-to-right, top-to-bottom.
The Blind-Spot Bug & Gated V+H Fix
Stacking $3\times 3$ Mask B kernels leaves an upper-right triangular blind spot of past pixels unreachable by $x_i$.
Gated PixelCNN pairs a Vertical Stack (rows above) with a Horizontal Stack (current row left).

Deformable Convolutions: Learned Sampling Offsets

Augment fixed grid offsets $\vec{p}_n$ with learned fractional shifts $\Delta\vec{p}_n$:
$y(\vec{p}_0) = \sum_{\vec{p}_n \in \mathcal{R}} w(\vec{p}_n)\, x(\vec{p}_0 + \vec{p}_n + \Delta\vec{p}_n)$
  • A tiny conv branch predicts $2k^2$ offsets $(\Delta x_n, \Delta y_n)$ from the feature map.
  • Bilinear interpolation evaluates $x$ at fractional coordinates — making $\nabla_{\Delta\vec{p}_n}$ smooth for AD!
Dai et al. (ICCV 2017): Deformable Convolutional Networks

Take Home Points

  • Inductive Biases Beat Brute Force: Spatial locality + Toeplitz weight sharing cut dense $O(H^2 W^2)$ parameters to $O(k^2)$ and guarantee translation equivariance $T_{\Delta}(\bm{X} \star \bm{K}) = (T_{\Delta}\bm{X}) \star \bm{K}$.
  • Convolution Geometry & Receptive Fields: Output dimension obeys $\lfloor (H_{\text{in}} + 2p - d(k-1) - 1)/s \rfloor + 1$; pooling sacrifices spatial resolution, whereas dilated convolutions grow context exponentially ($2^\ell$) at full resolution.
  • Skip Connections Tame Reverse-Mode AD: Highway gates ($b_T \ll 0$) and ResNet identity shortcuts ($\vec{x} + \mathcal{F}(\vec{x})$) inject $+\bm{I}$ into the backward Jacobian, smoothing loss landscapes across $100+$ layers.
  • Dense Prediction Without Dense Layers: $1\times 1$, transposed ($\bm{W}^\top$), and dilated convolutions enable resolution-agnostic FCNs, encoder–decoder U-Nets, and $320\times$ leaner 3D MeshNets (Brainchop).
  • Modern Versatility: Causal masks (PixelCNN / WaveNet), learned fractional offsets (Deformable Conv), and depthwise-separable blocks (ConvNeXt, Diffusion U-Nets, SSMs) keep convolutions central to AI.
