In Lecture 9, MLE was easy because every data point came from a single known distribution family. In Lecture 16, we saw Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) and solved clustering heuristically with hard and soft K-means. Today we ask: why did we need a heuristic in Lecture 16 instead of just taking the derivative of the GMM log-likelihood? That question leads directly to Expectation Maximization.

Do we even need EM for GMM?

Gaussian Mixture Model

Likelihood: $\prob{p}{\vec{x}} = \sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k\prob{N}{\vec{x}|\vec{\mu}_k, \bm{\Sigma}_k}$
$$\begin{aligned} \mbox{for simplicity }\bm{\Sigma}_k & = \sigma^2 \bm{I}\\ \prob{p}{\vec{x}|y=k} & = \prob{N}{\vec{\mu}_k, \sigma^2 \bm{I}}\\ \prob{p}{y=k} & = \pi_k\\ \mbox{parameters: } & \vec{\mu}_1, \dots, \vec{\mu}_K, \; \sigma^2, \; \pi_1, \dots, \pi_K \end{aligned}$$
But do we even need the hidden variables?
Class: how else beside what we covered in the K-means lecture can we find the parameters? Why not direct Maximum Likelihood Estimation?

Maximum (Log) Likelihood Estimation

$$\ln \prob{p}{\bm{X}|\{\vec{\mu}_k\}, \{\bm{\Sigma}_k\}, \{\pi_k\}} = \sum_{n=1}^N \ln\left\{\sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k \prob{N}{\vec{x}_n|\vec{\mu}_k, \sigma_k^2\bm{I}} \right\}$$

Difficult to optimize

  • Remember the exponential family? $$\prob{p}{\vec{x}|\vec{\eta}} = \prob{h}{\vec{x}}\prob{g}{\vec{\eta}}e^{\vec{\eta}^T\prob{u}{\vec{x}}}$$
  • How easy the $\log$-likelihood was back then: $$\begin{aligned} \log{\cal L} & = \sum \log \prob{p}{\vec{x}|\vec{\eta}} = \sum \log \prob{h}{\vec{x}} + \sum \log \prob{g}{\vec{\eta}} + \sum \vec{\eta}^T\prob{u}{\vec{x}} \end{aligned}$$
  • But a mixture $\sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k \prob{p}{\vec{x}|\vec{\eta}_k}$ is not in the exponential family!
  • The sum inside the logarithm prevents $\ln$ from canceling $\exp$:
    $$\ln \prob{p}{\bm{X}| \{\vec{\mu}_k\}, \{\bm{\Sigma}_k\}, \{\pi_k\}} = \sum_{n=1}^N\ln\left\{\sum_{k=1}^K \pi_k\prob{N}{\vec{x}_n|\vec{\mu}_k, \sigma_k^2\bm{I}} \right\}$$

Another problem (common to MLE)

  • Suppose $K = 2$
  • Suppose one component centers right on a single data point: $\vec{\mu}_k = \vec{x}_i$
  • What is going to happen with our MLE? $${\cal N}(x_i | \mu_k, \sigma_k^2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma_k^2}} e^{-\frac{(x_i-\mu_k)^2}{2\sigma_k^2}}$$
$${\cal N}(x_i \mid \mu_k = x_i, \sigma_k^2) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma_k} \to \infty \quad \text{as} \quad \sigma_k \to 0$$

GMM estimation: a hack

Mixture of 2 Gaussians

  • $$\prob{p}{x_n|\mu_1, \mu_2, \sigma} = \sum_{k=1}^2 \pi_k\prob{N}{x_n|\mu_k, \sigma^2}$$
  • $$\prob{p}{x_n|\mu_1, \mu_2, \sigma} = \sum_{k=1}^2 \pi_k\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}\exp{\left(-\frac{(x_n-\mu_k)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)}$$
  • Let's assume $\pi_1 = \pi_2 = 1/2$ and pack parameters into $\vec{\theta} = \{\mu_1, \mu_2, \sigma\}$

Mixture of 2 Gaussians

assuming known $\mu_k$ and $\sigma$

$$\begin{aligned} \prob{P}{k=1|x_n,\vec{\theta}} & = \frac{1}{1 + \exp{\left[-(w_1x_n+w_0)\right]}}\\ \prob{P}{k=2|x_n,\vec{\theta}} & = \frac{1}{1 + \exp{\left[+(w_1x_n+w_0)\right]}} \end{aligned}$$
recall logistic regression and softmax!

Mixture of 2 Gaussians

assuming known $\sigma$ but not $\mu_k$

  • $$\prob{p}{x_n|\mu_1, \mu_2, \sigma} = \sum_{k=1}^2 \pi_k\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}\exp{\left(-\frac{(x_n-\mu_k)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)}$$
  • $$\prob{p}{\bm{X}|\mu_1, \mu_2, \sigma} = \underset{n}{\prod} \prob{p}{x_n|\mu_1, \mu_2, \sigma}$$
  • Taking the derivative of the log-likelihood ${\cal L} = \ln \prob{p}{\bm{X}|\vec{\theta}}$: $$\frac{\partial {\cal L}}{\partial \mu_k} = \sum_n \prob{P}{k|x_n,\vec{\theta}} \frac{x_n-\mu_k}{\sigma^2}$$

Mixture of 2 Gaussians

assuming known $\sigma$ but not $\mu_k$

  • First derivative of the log-likelihood: $$\frac{\partial {\cal L}}{\partial \mu_k} = \sum_n \prob{P}{k|x_n,\vec{\theta}} \frac{x_n-\mu_k}{\sigma^2}$$
  • Approximate second derivative$^{*}$: $$\frac{\partial^2 {\cal L}}{\partial \mu_k^2} \approx -\sum_n \prob{P}{k|x_n,\vec{\theta}} \frac{1}{\sigma^2}$$
$^{*}$Hack: Ignore $\frac{\partial}{\partial \mu_k}\prob{P}{k|x_n,\vec{\theta}}$ (pretend responsibilities are constant!)

Mixture of 2 Gaussians

assuming known $\sigma$ but not $\mu_k$

Using the good old Newton-Raphson update: $\mu_k \leftarrow \mu_k - \frac{\partial {\cal L}}{\partial \mu_k}\Big/\frac{\partial^2 {\cal L}}{\partial \mu_k^2}$
Yields the weighted average update: $$\mu_k = \frac{\sum_n \prob{P}{k|x_n,\vec{\theta}} x_n}{\sum_n \prob{P}{k|x_n,\vec{\theta}} }$$

Compare to soft k-means: Iterations

Update
  • Re-estimate the $K$ cluster centers (aka the centroid or mean), by assuming the memberships found above are correct: $$\begin{aligned} \hat{\vec{\mu}}_k &= \frac{\underset{n}{\sum}r_k^n \vec{x}_n}{R_k}, \qquad R_k = \underset{n}{\sum} r_k^n \end{aligned}$$

GMMs and k-means

  • Responsibilities $r_k^n = \prob{P}{k|\vec{x}_n,\vec{\theta}}$ are posteriors over latent cluster assignments
  • Update is maximization of the likelihood holding responsibilities fixed
Both "hacky" approaches to solve a latent variable problem are instances of a rigorous general principle:
Expectation Maximization (EM): a meta-algorithm

Convexity

Theorem:
If $f(x)$ is twice differentiable on $[a,b]$ and $f^{\prime \prime}(x) \ge 0$ on $[a,b]$ then $f(x)$ is convex on $[a,b]$.

Convexity of logarithm

Theorem:
$-\ln(x)$ is strictly convex on $(0, \infty)$   $\left(\text{since } \frac{d^2}{dx^2}[-\ln x] = \frac{1}{x^2} > 0\right)$

Jensen's inequality

Theorem:
Let $f$ be a convex function on an interval $I$. If $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n \in I$ with $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_n \ge 0$ and $\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_i=1$: $$f\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_ix_i\right) \le \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i f(x_i)$$

Thanks to Jensen's inequality

Since $-\ln(x)$ is strictly convex, $\ln(x)$ is strictly concave, which reverses the inequality: $$\ln\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\lambda_ix_i\right) \ge \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i \ln{(x_i)}$$
In expectation notation (for $\lambda_i = q(z_i)$):   $\ln \EE_{q}[X] \ge \EE_{q}[\ln X]$, with equality iff $x_1 = x_2 = \dots = x_n$.
Now we are ready!

Jensen's Inequality: Log of Sum vs. Sum of Logs

On the horizontal axis, the weighted average is E[X] = lambda*x_1 + (1 - lambda)*x_2. 3. Look vertically above E[X]: - The GREEN dot on the blue curve has height ln(lambda*x_1 + (1 - lambda)*x_2) = ln E[X]. This is the intractable LOG-OF-SUMS in our mixture log-likelihood. - The ORANGE dot on the straight secant chord has height lambda*ln(x_1) + (1 - lambda)*ln(x_2) = E[ln X]. This is the tractable SUM-OF-LOGS where ln acts directly on each term. 4. Because ln(x) is strictly concave (curves downward), the blue curve always arches ABOVE the straight secant line connecting (x_1, ln x_1) and (x_2, ln x_2). Thus replacing the log-of-sums with the sum-of-logs gives a guaranteed lower bound! 5. Click "Tight Bound: x_1 = x_2 (Gap = 0)": when x_1 = x_2, both endpoints of the secant chord merge into a single point on the curve and the vertical red Jensen Gap vanishes to 0. In the E-step on the next slides, we choose q(z) = P(z | X, theta_n) specifically so all terms x_i inside the log become equal at theta = theta_n, making the lower bound touch the true log-likelihood!

Derivation of EM algorithm

  • Our goal is to maximize the log-likelihood function: $$\prob{L}{\vec{\theta}} = \ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{\theta}}$$
  • Equivalently, if at step $n$ we have $\vec{\theta}_n$, we want $\vec{\theta}$ such that: $$\prob{L}{\vec{\theta}} \gt \prob{L}{\vec{\theta}_n}$$
  • Yet equivalently, we want to maximize the improvement: $$\prob{L}{\vth} - \prob{L}{\vec{\theta}_n} = \ln\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{\theta}} - \ln\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{\theta}_n}$$
  • Looks difficult!

Introducing latent random variables $\vec{z}$

  • By marginalization over the hidden variables $\vec{z}$: $$\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{\theta}} = \sum_{\vec{z}} \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{z}, \vth}\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\vth}$$
  • Our objective difference: $$\prob{L}{\vth} - \prob{L}{\vec{\theta}_n} = \ln\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{\theta}} - \ln\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{\theta}_n}$$
  • Becomes: $$\prob{L}{\vth} - \prob{L}{\vec{\theta}_n} = \ln \sum_{\vec{z}} \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{z}, \vth}\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\vth} - \ln\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{\theta}_n}$$

Modifying the Objective via Jensen's Inequality

$$\prob{L}{\vth} - \prob{L}{\vth_n} = \ln \sum_{\vec{z}} \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{z}, \vth}\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\vth} - \ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth_n}$$
1. Multiply inside the sum by $1 = \frac{\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n}}{\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n}}$ (where $\sum_{\vec{z}} \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n} = 1$ acts as $\lambda_i$): $$= \ln \sum_{\vec{z}} \color{#268bd2}{\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n}} \left( \frac{\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{z}, \vth}\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\vth}}{\color{#268bd2}{\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n}}} \right) - \ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth_n}$$
2. Apply Jensen's Inequality $\ln\left(\sum \lambda_i u_i\right) \ge \sum \lambda_i \ln(u_i)$: $$\color{#cb4b16}{\ge} \sum_{\vec{z}} \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n} \ln \left( \frac{\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{z}, \vth}\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\vth}}{\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n}} \right) - \ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth_n}$$
3. Absorb $\ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth_n} = \sum_{\vec{z}} \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X},\vth_n}\ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth_n}$ into the logarithm: $$= \sum_{\vec{z}} \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n} \ln \left( \frac{\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{z}, \vth}\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\vth}}{\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n}\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth_n}} \right) \;\def\; \mathbf{\Delta(\vth|\vth_n)}$$

Define the lower bound

$$\begin{aligned} {\cal L}(\vth|\vth_n) & \def \prob{L}{\vth_n} + \Delta(\vth|\vth_n)\\ \prob{L}{\vth} & \ge {\cal L}(\vth|\vth_n) \end{aligned}$$

Tightness: When does $\prob{L}{\vth} = {\cal L}(\vth|\vth_n)$?

Evaluate the surrogate lower bound at the current iterate $\vth = \vth_n$:

$${\cal L}(\vth_n|\vth_n) = \prob{L}{\vth_n} + \Delta(\vth_n|\vth_n) = \prob{L}{\vth_n} + \sum_{\vec{z}} \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n} \ln \frac{\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{z}, \vth_n}\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\vth_n}}{\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n}\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth_n}}$$
By Bayes' rule, both numerator and denominator equal the joint distribution $\prob{P}{\bm{X}, \vec{z}|\vth_n}$: $$= \prob{L}{\vth_n} + \sum_{\vec{z}} \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n} \ln \frac{\prob{P}{\bm{X}, \vec{z}|\vth_n}}{\prob{P}{\bm{X}, \vec{z}|\vth_n}} = \prob{L}{\vth_n} + \sum_{\vec{z}} \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n} \underbrace{\ln 1}_{0}$$
$${\cal L}(\vth_n|\vth_n) = \prob{L}{\vth_n} \quad \text{(The lower bound touches } \prob{L}{\vth} \text{ tangentially at } \vth_n\text{!)}$$

What happens when we optimize ${\cal L}(\vth|\vth_n)$?

Simplifying the M-Step Objective: The $Q$-Function

$$\vth_{n+1} = \underset{\vth}{\argmax} \left\{ {\cal L}(\vth|\vth_n) \right\} = \underset{\vth}{\argmax} \left\{ \cancel{\prob{L}{\vth_n}} + \sum_{\vec{z}} \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n} \ln \frac{\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{z}, \vth}\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\vth}}{\cancel{\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth_n}\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n}}} \right\}$$
Drop all terms constant w.r.t. $\vth$ and combine $\prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vec{z},\vth}\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\vth} = \prob{P}{\bm{X},\vec{z}|\vth}$: $$\vth_{n+1} = \underset{\vth}{\argmax} \left\{ \sum_{\vec{z}} \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n} \ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}, \vec{z}|\vth} \right\} = \underset{\vth}{\argmax} \left\{ \underbrace{\EE_{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n}\!\left[ \ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}, \vec{z}|\vth} \right]}_{Q(\vth|\vth_n)} \right\}$$
1. Expectation Step (E-Step)
Compute posterior distribution of latent variables $q^{(n)}(\vec{z}) = \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth_n}$ and form the expected complete-data log-likelihood: $$Q(\vth|\vth_n) = \EE_{\vec{z}|\bm{X},\vth_n}\!\left[\ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}, \vec{z}|\vth}\right]$$
2. Maximization Step (M-Step)
Update parameters by maximizing the expected complete-data log-likelihood: $$\vth_{n+1} = \underset{\vth}{\argmax} \; Q(\vth|\vth_n)$$ (Inside $Q$, $\ln$ acts directly on the complete joint $\prob{P}{\bm{X},\vec{z}|\vth}$!)

Kullback-Leibler divergence

$D_{\text{KL}}(p \parallel q) = \int p(x) \ln \frac{p(x)}{q(x)} dx \ge 0$   (asymmetric! $D_{\text{KL}}(p\parallel q) \ne D_{\text{KL}}(q\parallel p)$)

Forward vs. Reverse KL Divergence

Optimize q(x):
D_KL(p∥q): -- D_KL(q∥p): --
Compare Forward KL vs Reverse KL: 1. In Forward KL D_KL(p || q), wherever p(x) > 0, q(x) must be > 0 or the KL diverges to infinity. Thus q(x) spreads out to cover both modes ("mass-covering"), placing its peak in the zero-density valley! 2. In Reverse KL D_KL(q || p) — which is what EM and Variational Inference minimize — wherever p(x) is 0, q(x) is forced to 0 ("zero-forcing / mode-seeking"), locking cleanly onto a single mode!

Yet Another View of EM: ELBO & Free Energy

For any distribution $q(\vec{z})$ over the latent variables, decompose $\ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth} = \sum_{\vec{z}} q(\vec{z}) \ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth}$: $$\ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth} = \underbrace{\sum_{\vec{z}} q(\vec{z}) \ln \frac{\prob{P}{\bm{X}, \vec{z}|\vth}}{q(\vec{z})}}_{\text{ELBO / Free Energy } {\cal F}(q, \vth)} + \underbrace{D_{\text{KL}}\!\left(q(\vec{z}) \;\middle\|\; \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth}\right)}_{\ge\, 0}$$
Expanding the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) separates energy and entropy: $${\cal F}(q, \vth) = \sum_{\vec{z}} q(\vec{z}) \ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}, \vec{z}|\vth} - \sum_{\vec{z}} q(\vec{z}) \ln q(\vec{z}) = \mathbf{Q(\vth | q) + H(q)} \le \ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth}$$
EM = Coordinate Ascent on ${\cal F}(q, \vth)$:
  • E-Step (fix $\vth_t$, optimize $q$):
    $q^{(t)}(\vec{z}) = \argmax_q {\cal F}(q, \vth_t) = \argmin_q D_{\text{KL}}(q \parallel P(\vec{z}|\bm{X},\vth_t))$
    Exact solution: $q^{(t)}(\vec{z}) = \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X},\vth_t} \implies D_{\text{KL}} = 0$!
  • M-Step (fix $q^{(t)}$, optimize $\vth$):
    $\vth_{t+1} = \argmax_{\vth} {\cal F}(q^{(t)}, \vth) = \argmax_{\vth} Q(\vth|\vth_t)$
Bridge to Variational Inference (VI & VAEs):

What if the true posterior $\prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X}, \vth}$ is intractable?

Restrict $q(\vec{z}) \in {\cal Q}$ to a tractable family (e.g., factorized mean-field or a neural encoder $q_{\bm{\phi}}(\vec{z}|\vec{x})$) and maximize $\text{ELBO}(q, \vth)$ directly!

This identity is one of the most important equations in modern machine learning: Log marginal likelihood = ELBO + KL(q || posterior). In classical EM, we can compute the exact posterior P(z|X,theta), so the E-step sets KL = 0. In Variational Inference and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), the exact posterior is intractable, so we restrict q(z) to a tractable family and maximize the ELBO!

EM for General Gaussian Mixture Models

Why Complete Data Makes GMM Estimation Easy

Introduce a one-hot latent indicator $\vec{z}_n = (z_{n1}, \dots, z_{nK}) \in \{0,1\}^K$ with $\sum_{k=1}^K z_{nk} = 1$: $$\prob{P}{\vec{x}_n, \vec{z}_n | \vth} = \prod_{k=1}^K \left[ \pi_k \prob{N}{\vec{x}_n | \vec{\mu}_k, \bm{\Sigma}_k} \right]^{z_{nk}}$$
Take the logarithm of the complete data likelihood: the product turns into a sum, and the exponent $z_{nk}$ comes out front — no log-of-sums! $$\ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}, \bm{Z} | \vth} = \sum_{n=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^K z_{nk} \left[ \ln \pi_k + \ln \prob{N}{\vec{x}_n | \vec{\mu}_k, \bm{\Sigma}_k} \right]$$
Take the expectation $\EE_{\bm{Z}|\bm{X},\vth^{(t)}}$: since $\ln \prob{P}{\bm{X},\bm{Z}|\vth}$ is linear in $z_{nk}$, we simply replace $z_{nk}$ with its posterior expectation $\gamma_{nk} \def \EE[z_{nk}|\vec{x}_n, \vth^{(t)}] = \prob{P}{z_{nk}=1|\vec{x}_n, \vth^{(t)}}$: $$Q(\vth | \vth^{(t)}) = \sum_{n=1}^N \sum_{k=1}^K \color{#cb4b16}{\gamma_{nk}} \left[ \ln \pi_k - \frac{1}{2}\ln|\bm{\Sigma}_k| - \frac{1}{2}(\vec{x}_n - \vec{\mu}_k)^T \bm{\Sigma}_k^{-1} (\vec{x}_n - \vec{\mu}_k) \right] + \text{const}$$

EM Algorithm for General Multivariate GMMs

1. E-Step: Responsibilities

For each data point $n=1,\dots,N$ and component $k=1,\dots,K$, evaluate the posterior membership probability:

$$\gamma_{nk} = \frac{\pi_k^{(t)} \prob{N}{\vec{x}_n \mid \vec{\mu}_k^{(t)}, \bm{\Sigma}_k^{(t)}}}{\sum_{j=1}^K \pi_j^{(t)} \prob{N}{\vec{x}_n \mid \vec{\mu}_j^{(t)}, \bm{\Sigma}_j^{(t)}}}$$

Define effective point count assigned to cluster $k$: $$N_k \def \sum_{n=1}^N \gamma_{nk} \quad \left(\sum_{k=1}^K N_k = N\right)$$

2. M-Step: Weighted MLE

Maximize $Q(\vth|\vth^{(t)})$ (with Lagrange multiplier $\sum_k \pi_k = 1$):

$$\vec{\mu}_k^{(t+1)} = \frac{1}{N_k} \sum_{n=1}^N \gamma_{nk} \vec{x}_n$$ $$\bm{\Sigma}_k^{(t+1)} = \frac{1}{N_k} \sum_{n=1}^N \gamma_{nk} (\vec{x}_n - \vec{\mu}_k^{(t+1)})(\vec{x}_n - \vec{\mu}_k^{(t+1)})^T$$ $$\pi_k^{(t+1)} = \frac{N_k}{N}$$

GMMs vs. K-Means & Practical Engineering

1. K-Means as a Limit of GMM EM:
Fix $\bm{\Sigma}_k = \sigma^2 \bm{I}$ and $\pi_k = 1/K$. As $\sigma^2 \to 0$, the softmax responsibilities $\gamma_{nk}$ collapse to $\{0, 1\}$ indicator variables, recovering Hard K-Means!
2. Quadratic vs. Linear Boundaries:
With general $\bm{\Sigma}_k$, the log posterior ratio $\ln \frac{P(y=i|\vec{x})}{P(y=j|\vec{x})} = \vec{x}^T\bm{W}\vec{x} + \vec{w}^T\vec{x} + c$ yields quadratic decision boundaries instead of linear Voronoi cells.
3. Choosing $K$ (Model Selection):
Training log-likelihood $\ln P(\bm{X}|\hat{\vth})$ increases monotonically with $K$. Select $K$ via held-out validation likelihood or BIC ($p$ = # of parameters): $$\text{BIC}(K) = -2\ln P(\bm{X}|\hat{\vth}) + p \ln N$$
4. Initialization & Regularization:
Initialize $\vec{\mu}_k$ with K-Means / K-Means++ across multiple random restarts, and add $\bm{\Sigma}_k \leftarrow \bm{\Sigma}_k + \epsilon\bm{I}$ ($\epsilon \approx 10^{-6}$) to prevent singularity collapse.

Take Home Points

  • Why Direct MLE Fails on Mixtures: The log-of-sums $\ln \sum_k \pi_k \prob{N}{\vec{x}_n|\vec{\mu}_k, \bm{\Sigma}_k}$ breaks exponential-family decoupling, couples all parameters nonlinearly, and suffers from variance-collapse singularities ($\sigma_k \to 0 \implies \mathcal{L} \to \infty$).
  • Latent Variables Restore Tractability: Introducing unobserved cluster assignments $\vec{z}$ turns the complete-data log-likelihood $\ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}, \bm{Z}|\vth}$ back into a tractable sum of logs.
  • Jensen's Inequality & The ELBO Surrogate: By concavity of $\ln$, EM constructs a tight lower bound ${\cal L}(\vth|\vth_t) = {\cal F}(q, \vth) = \ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth} - D_{\text{KL}}(q(\vec{z}) \parallel \prob{P}{\vec{z}|\bm{X},\vth})$ tangent at $\vth_t$.
  • EM as Coordinate Ascent: The E-step computes posterior responsibilities $\gamma_{nk} = \prob{P}{z_n=k|\vec{x}_n,\vth_t}$ (closing the KL gap to zero); the M-step maximizes expected complete-data log-likelihood $Q(\vth|\vth_t)$ via weighted MLE.
  • Monotonicity & GMM vs. K-Means: EM monotonically increases $\ln \prob{P}{\bm{X}|\vth}$ without a step-size hyperparameter, generalizes K-means ($\sigma^2 \to 0$ limit) to full covariance ellipsoids $\bm{\Sigma}_k$, and uses K-means++ restarts + BIC/validation for selecting $K$.
